The ensemble

Five players, one screen: how to play Current together, sound only, on the pad.

For the Frost Electronic Music Ensemble, Frost School of Music, University of Miami. Performance theme: water, 8 November 2026.

Dimitry Saïd Chamy · Artist, Designer, Cultural Producer

Deeplow and slow: bubbles, a whale Surfacedrops, bubbles, lapping Shoregulls, waves, the lighthouse Walkersteps, splashes, sand Beachcomberglass and bottles The screenfor the projector: no sound

Five players and one screen share a room. Each player has a part, its own sounds and its own color. The screen is a laptop on the projector: it shows the field, and every player’s finger pulls the current there, in that part’s color.

  • Your sound comes from your device. Phone, tablet or laptop: plug it into the desk (headphone out or an interface). The screen makes no sound.

  • The screen leads. The scene it shows, picked by hand, by the robot or by a sequence, is the scene everyone plays. Its tempo is the room’s tempo too: Walker’s steps, Shore’s lighthouse and every sound left on a pad land on its beat.

  • You see only your own touch. Everyone sees the same picture on the projector, and on your own device only your part.

  • Open your link. Each link above opens Current with its part and joins room FEMEH2. Nothing to install, nothing to type. The pill at the top says who you are and whether you reach the screen.

Every part has five sounds, numbered 1 to 5. The first three or four play from your finger (tap, drag, hold, touch); the others start on keys: the number keys, sounds you leave on the pad, the Corners map and the halo play them. Give any sound any gesture in the mixer.

Two shelves: set up, then play

On a laptop the player view is mostly pad. The upper shelf is the set-up, for before the piece: the map, what the pad is (wall, stage, floor) and where zero sits, the key and scale, notes in the key or free, how taps land, the field behind, and every sound there is: tap one to add it to your part (up to nine), tap an added one to take it out. Ready folds it away; Set up brings it back.

The lower strip is the live part: your sounds as chips (a dot plays once, a ring is held, ticks play on the beat), your part’s level, mute and panic (lets go of everything held). Tap a chip to play it; hold it (or right-click) for its ring: level, timbre, grain and pitch (drag up or down on each), what plays it (a ring of its own: tap, drag, hold, touch, keys), leave it, play it, take it out; drag it onto the pad to leave it there. Mix, Map and Keys still open the panel beside the pad; tap the lit one again to fold it.

The pad is the screen’s picture. It has the projector’s shape, 16:9: where you touch here is where your sound lands there.

  • Two axes, four edges, four corners. A is the top, B the bottom, C the left, D the right. The corners are E and F at the top, H and G at the bottom. Each edge shows what it plays there: with the Water map, up is higher and right is wetter.

  • Maps. Water: pitch up, wetness across. Grain: grain up, wetness across. Timbre: pitch up, timbre across. Stage: pitch up, the stage across, left to right. Corners: a sound in each corner and the bed in the middle; while you touch, they all play, each as loud as your finger is near it. Choose which sound sits in which corner.

  • Wall, stage or floor. The same pad, named three ways: a wall (top, bottom, left, right), a stage (upstage, downstage, stage right, stage left) or a floor (far, near, left, right). Zero sits at an edge (0 to 100) or in the centre (−100 to 100).

  • In tune. The screen sets the room’s key (C major pentatonic to start). Your pitched sounds (the whale, drops, the lighthouse, the bottle and the glass) land on its nearest note, and the key’s notes are named up the right edge of the pad, the root brighter; the note under your finger shows under the pad (♪ E3). Bubbles, sand, splashes and waves are noise and stay free. Map › In tune, in time turns notes to free pitch; alone, without the screen, you choose the key there.

  • In time. Four lights at the top count the beats of the bar (the first one bigger), with the tempo and, after a count-in, the bar: bar 3 · 76 bpm. When the screen counts in, four big numbers count down on your pad and bar one starts for everyone. Taps land (in Map) can wait for the next beat or ⅛, so a tap is always in time (a held sound, like the bottle, still starts at once).

  • What you see. Your trail and finger in your part’s color, a ring where each sound plays with its name beside it, and the numbers under the pad: where you are and what that means.

Leave a sound and it keeps playing there, on the beat, while you play something else. Up to twelve per player.

The halo

The halo is a ring, like the Touch ring, with the sound itself in the middle. The words under the buttons show as it opens, then fade once you know them.

Touch anywhere else, or press Esc, to close it. With a keyboard, the arrow keys go round it and Enter presses.

Left sounds play on the room’s tempo, the one the screen keeps. Without a room tempo, Current counts its own quiet 76 a minute from your first touch.

For whoever runs the projector. Open the screen link on the laptop connected to the projector.

  • Clean screen. On the bar’s Room shelf, Clean screen goes full screen and hides every control; nothing reloads. It is the same as Ready to perform in the Ensemble section. Tap it again, or tap with three fingers, to bring the controls back.

  • Clean output. On a laptop with two screens, Clean output opens the stage alone in its own window for the projector; the laptop keeps the controls. If the browser blocks the window, allow pop-ups for the site and tap again.

  • Key and count-in. In the Ensemble section, the screen picks the room’s key and scale; every player’s pitched sounds follow within two seconds. Count in (there, or on the bar’s Room shelf) counts four beats on every pad and starts bar one together. With no tempo playing, it starts one at 76 a minute.

  • Scenes and tempo. Pick scenes by hand, or let the robot or a sequence play them; every player’s device follows. Tap a tempo, or take it from Ableton Link, and the room plays on it.

  • Charge everything, and turn on Do Not Disturb.
  • Open your link on the device you will play, and tap the pad once to wake the sound.
  • Check the pill: it should say you reach the screen.
  • Set your level in Mix, and agree who holds the bed in each section: five beds at once is a wall.
  • Tap Ready (or R) so nothing pops up while you play.
  • On iPhone, Guided Access keeps the screen in Current.

Your mix, map and the sounds you left are kept on your device, for your part, until you tap Reset the part.
