— OSC in

Not connected.

—

Arena cannot reach a browser directly — nothing can open a UDP socket from a page — so bridge.py listens for OSC and passes it on. In Arena: Preferences → OSC → Output, host 127.0.0.1, port 9000. Then Learn, move a fader in Arena, and click the dial it should drive. This only works on localhost; the artifact cannot reach your machine.

3

Video records H.264 MP4 where the browser can, WebM only if it cannot — but it is paced to the wall clock, so 2160 may stretch. PNG sequence writes straight into a folder you choose: frame accurate at any size, with alpha, and it is what Alley, Arena and After Effects want — but folder access is refused to an embedded page, so it needs localhost. Save this frame writes one PNG at the export resolution and works anywhere.

— how a glyph may arrive

Every motion describes where a glyph sits when it is away, so one whose away-position is above both enters from above and leaves back upward. Carry on through flips that — a letter that fell in from the top keeps going and leaves out the bottom, rather than retreating the way it came. Exits fall pulls the chosen share downward whichever way they were headed, keeping the spin, turn or scale the motion brought with it. Gravity wins over both.

American is the background plate: drawn normally, with the blanks added over it. That is what keeps the small words in front — additive over a plate brightens where they cross. Turn this on and it joins the pile-up instead. Stomp drops it and squashes it again while it is standing still — the landing it already made on arrival, repeated. It is drawn, not counted: most holds get none, some get one, a few get several, and they land where they land rather than on a beat. A regular count read as a metronome; this reads as a thing losing its temper. Pulse is the quiet one underneath — a few breaths in scale, no impact and no travel — and it comes and goes on its own odds.

Everything random is seeded — except the voice

Which way each glyph arrives and leaves, its colour, whether it doubles: all from mulberry32 keyed on line, actor and glyph, the same generator the Builder uses for arrangement #2026. The microphone is the one live input, and it is live on purpose.

How the fit gives way

Overlap first, then the blanks shrink, and the spine shrinks last — the fixed word is the one thing that never changes, so it is the last thing allowed to move.

The coda never runs on a loop

The homage at the end of the piece is said by a person to people who are present. It is deliberately absent from this script and from anything that plays unattended.
