Play the field. Current is a living field: thousands of particles that move like water, smoke or a flock, and answer you. Touch it and it parts around your finger. Give it sound and it dances: a beat makes it surge, a drone makes it drift. Hand it a drawing, a word, a photo or your camera, and it flows along it.

It plays like an instrument, not like software. It opens in a browser on a phone, a tablet, a laptop or a projector, with nothing to install, so anyone can pick it up and play in the first minute: someone who has never made music, a student, a seasoned performer. The further you go, the more it opens: your own sounds, your own patterns, scenes that change by themselves, a whole ensemble in time.

Underneath is G-code, the language that tells plotters, lasers and routers where to move. Current turns each cut into a current the particles follow. Whatever plays (a beat, a bytebeat, your track, the microphone, or the sounds in your pack) is split into five signals, and the field answers them.

How the field hears

SignalWhat it isWhat it does by default
bass30 to 150 Hzpushes the field
mid150 to 2,000 Hzcolors and line work, in scenes that link it
high2,000 to 12,000 Hzsparkle and detail, in scenes that link it
levelall three togetherlouder makes the particles faster
beata sudden jump in the bassthe field surges for a moment

Each signal rests at zero and rises with the sound, with a slow automatic gain so quiet material still reads. The field always hears the dry sound: the reverb you add with wet changes what the audience hears, not how the particles move. Practical upshot: if you want the field to jump, give it bass transients; if you want it to drift, give it a steady bed.

Every sound you bring in has a role. You choose it when it comes in and can change it at any time in the pack sheet. A pack can set a role for each of its sounds (On the field is "role": "pad" in a pack).

Touch

Your finger on the stage plays it. A tap strikes it; a hold sustains a sound that holds. Left to right pans it.

Drips, plucks, bowed glass you can rub.

On the beat

It strikes on the beat grid: every beat, 2 and 4, the off-beats, once a bar, or eighths. The grid follows Current's beat or your tap tempo (100 bpm if nothing sets one).

A low pulse, rain ticks, a clock.

Bed

It plays under everything while the sound is on. A sound that holds keeps holding (loop it); a short one repeats at its own length.

A seamless loop of water, a drone, room tone.

Pattern

The drawing is the sequencer. On the grid it walks the G-code's cuts in order, and each strike is panned to where its cut sits. Change the pattern and the phrase changes.

Pitched drops, a melody that wanders with the drawing.

On the field

The sound stands on the field as a dot in its own color. Across is left and right; height is pitch, up to an octave higher at the top and an octave lower at the bottom. A short sound repeats; a held sound holds for as long as its dot is there.

Drag a sound onto the field from the Pack tab, or tap its dot there. Drag a dot and let go: it repeats your journey. The particles flow round every dot, harder while it sounds and moves. Drag it to the edge to take it off; a long press opens its menu (smooth the journey, hold still). Ready-made: Tide pool, Two gulls, Undertow.

A Pro feature, open to try during the beta. In the lab build only, and the name is provisional.

Every touch sound that is switched on plays together on each tap, so keep one or two touch sounds on at a time and switch the rest off with their check mark.

  1. Open works.chamy.xyz/current and tap Launch. On a phone, add it to the home screen for a full screen.

  2. Tap the speaker on the bar to turn the sound on. A beat starts and the field moves to it.

  3. Hold the speaker to open the sound shelf, then tap pack. The Pack sheet opens.

  4. Tap Import… and pick one of your recordings (wav, mp3, ogg or m4a). Pick several at once if you like. On a laptop, Folder… takes a whole folder, and you can also drop files on the open sheet.

  5. Choose how it plays: Touch, On the beat, Bed or Pattern, then Add. A file shorter than 3 seconds comes in as a one-shot; 3 seconds or longer comes in as a loop that holds.

  6. Press the play triangle beside its name to hear it alone.

  7. Close the sheet and play: tap and hold on the stage for touch sounds; the others play by themselves.

  8. To hear only your sounds, tap the beat off in the sound shelf. The field keeps listening to your pack.

  9. Open pack again to change roles, timing (every beat, 2 and 4, off-beats, once a bar, eighths), or switch sounds on and off.

  10. When it feels right, tap Save as a pack. With recordings in it you get a pack .zip, ready to share.

Want a head start? Import the Frost Water starter pack: eight water sounds, two for each role, half recorded and half synthesized, with their roles already set.

Bring your own sounds and the sounds of the people you play for: field recordings, voices, instruments, your Ableton and Max patches. Current decodes them in the browser and keeps them on that device, so they are there the next time you open it.

One-shots and loops

A one-shot is a single event: a drip, a hit, a word. Trim it tight at the front and let its tail ring out naturally to silence. It suits touch, beat and pattern.

A loop repeats. Make the end flow into the start: cut at zero crossings, keep the level steady across the seam, and if it carries reverb, render the tail back into the start (Ableton's Render as Loop does this). Loops suit bed, and a held touch sound.

Recordings are kept per device and per browser. Importing on your phone does not put them on your laptop: bring the pack zip to each device you play on.

Ableton Live

  1. One-shots: in the clip's Sample box, switch Warp off so the audio plays at its own speed and pitch. Trim the clip to the sound.

  2. Select the clip and choose Consolidate (Cmd/Ctrl+J). Live writes a new file into the project's Samples/Processed/Consolidate folder; with effects or an instrument on the track, use Freeze and Flatten first so they are printed in.

  3. Or render: set the loop brace over the region, then File › Export Audio/Video (Cmd/Ctrl+Shift+R).

  4. In the export dialog: Rendered Track = the one track you want (or Selected Tracks Only), File Type WAV, Sample Rate 48000, Bit Depth 16 or 24, Normalize off.

  5. Loops: turn on Render as Loop, so reverb and delay tails wrap back into the start and the loop joins without a seam.

  6. Check the level on the master with a loudness meter (Youlean Loudness Meter is free): one-shots peak near −1 dBFS, loops near −18 LUFS. Adjust with a Utility device before you export.

Max

  1. Set the sample rate in Options › Audio Status (48000 is a good choice).

  2. From a buffer: record into [buffer~ drip 2000 2] with [record~ drip 2], then send the buffer the message writewave (a save dialog opens) or writewave drip.wav to name it directly. crop trims the buffer to the part you want first.

  3. From a running patch: connect your signal to [sfrecord~ 2]. Send open and choose a name ending in .wav, then 1 to start and 0 to stop.

  4. For a loop, record a whole number of cycles of your patch, or crossfade the ends in an editor before export.

Any editor works for the last trim and fade: Audacity, Ocenaudio, Logic, Reaper. Then Import into Current, or put the files in a pack.

You only need this to set roles in advance, combine recordings with synthesized sounds, or tune a sound beyond what the pack sheet offers. A pack is an ordinary zip:

my-pack.zip
├── pack.json          what the zip is
├── instrument.json    the set: the sounds, and how each one plays
├── README.txt         optional: credits, notes
└── samples/
    ├── drip.wav
    └── harbor-loop.wav

pack.json

{
  "kind": "surface.pack",       // always this: it marks the zip as a studio pack
  "v": 1,
  "tool": "current",
  "id": "my-pack",              // lowercase and dashes; keeps your recordings apart from other packs
  "name": "My pack",            // what Current shows
  "by": "your name"
}

instrument.json, commented

The comments here are for reading. JSON itself has no comments, so delete them in your file, or copy the template's instrument.json, which uses "_how" fields as notes instead (anything starting with _ is ignored).

{
  "format": "instrument/1",     // a set of sounds (the studio's set format)
  "name": "My pack",
  "recipes": [                  // one entry per sound, each in the voice/0 format

    {                           // 1 · A ONE-SHOT RECORDING
      "format": "voice/0",
      "name": "drip",           // must match its key in "roles" below
      "hold": false,            // false: plays once, to the end of the file
      "trim": -3,               // level in dB, added to everything
      "layers": [{
        "source": "sample",
        "sample": "samples/drip.wav",   // the path inside the zip
        "rate": [0.8, 1.3]      // playback speed; [low, high] picks a new one each time
      }]
    },

    {                           // 2 · A LOOP THAT HOLDS
      "format": "voice/0",
      "name": "harbor",
      "hold": true,             // true: keeps going while held (a bed, or a finger down)
      "layers": [{
        "source": "sample",
        "sample": "samples/harbor-loop.wav",
        "loop": true,
        "start": { "time": 1.5, "gain": 1 },  // fade in over 1.5 s
        "end":   { "time": 2.5, "gain": 0 }   // fade out over 2.5 s when it stops
      }]
    },

    {                           // 3 · A SYNTHESIZED SOUND (no file at all)
      "format": "voice/0",
      "name": "glass bowl",
      "hold": true,
      "layers": [{
        "source": "sine",       // sine, triangle, square, sawtooth or noise
        "pitch": 660,           // Hz
        "partials": [[1, 1], [2.32, 0.2], [4.25, 0.07]],  // [ratio, level]: glass modes
        "start": { "time": 0.5, "gain": 0.18 },
        "hold":  { "time": 2, "gain": 0.22, "wobble": { "rate": 2.4, "depth": 0.25 } },
        "end":   { "time": 2.2, "gain": 0, "curve": "exp" }   // rings off, like struck glass
      }]
    },

    {                           // 4 · GRAINS: a recording fired as events
      "format": "voice/0",
      "name": "drip echo",
      "hold": false,
      "events": [{
        "during": "start",
        "count": 2,             // two drips…
        "gap": 0.16,            // …0.16 s apart…
        "fade": 0.4,            // …the second at 40 %
        "grain": { "source": "sample", "sample": "samples/drip.wav", "rate": [0.7, 1.6] }
      }]
    }
  ],

  "roles": {                    // how each sound plays when the pack is opened
    "drip":       { "role": "touch",   "on": true,  "level": 1 },
    "harbor":     { "role": "bed",     "on": true,  "level": 0.8 },
    "glass bowl": { "role": "touch",   "on": false },
    "drip echo":  { "role": "pattern", "every": "eighth", "on": true }
  }                             // every: beat · back (2 and 4) · off · bar · eighth
}

trim · levelrecipe · rolesdB on the recipe; 0 to 2 in roles

The starter pack's instrument.json has eight working examples of all of this. Sounds made in the studio's sound tool use the same format and open here too.

  1. In Current, open pack and tap Save as a pack. With recordings in your pack, it saves a pack .zip holding your sounds, their roles and the recordings. (Without recordings it saves a single .json.)

  2. Or build the folder by hand from the template and zip it. Put pack.json at the top of the zip; one folder around everything is fine too. On a Mac, right-click the folder › Compress.

  3. Send the zip any way you like: AirDrop, the ensemble's shared drive, a message.

  4. To open one, in Current: pack › Import… and choose the zip, or drop it anywhere on Current. Nobody unzips it. The sheet lists the sounds and anything left out, then Add.

Credit the people whose sounds you carry. Put their names in your README.txt and in each recipe's "about", and only share recordings you have permission to share.

  • One device, one voice. Each performer plays Current on their own device, with their own sound out to the house mix: headphone out or an audio interface into the desk, one channel or stereo pair each.

  • Leave room. Five beds at once is a wall. Agree who holds the bed in each section, and keep beds near −18 LUFS.

  • Play as one room. One person leads: in Current, open Index › Room (or press o), type a name and tap Make a room. A 6-character code appears, with copy link. Everyone else opens Room, types the code and taps Join, or opens the link.

    Each performer keeps their own sounds. The leader sets what the room follows: Free, Tempo or Scenes; each player chooses to follow the tempo, the scenes or both. Built-in beats, the tap grid and beat and pattern sounds land together; bytebeats and your own audio files do not re-align.

    If your speaker lags (Bluetooth), Nudge moves your beat up to 60 ms either way. The lead can be handed to anyone, and passes on by itself if the leader leaves.

  • Visual-only passages. The field moves only while your sound plays. To be seen and not heard, keep the sound on and have your channel turned down at the desk.

  • Before the show.

    • Charge everything.
    • Turn on Do Not Disturb.
    • Set the device volume.
    • Open your pack on the device you will play.
    • On iPhone, Guided Access keeps the screen in Current.

  • Projection. On a laptop, Clean output opens the stage alone in its own window for a projector, while the controls stay on your screen.

  • Water. Current also has its own water sounds (Shallows, Deep, Open sea, Rain on the roof, Stream, Hydrophone, Harbor, Pod) and a ripple surface over the stage. Try them under your pack.
